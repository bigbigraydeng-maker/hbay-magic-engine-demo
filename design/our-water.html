HBay’s source story begins beneath the Heretaunga Plains in Hawke’s Bay. River gravels, sand, silt and clay form a layered landscape, built as rivers and coastlines changed over time.

The supplied extracts from the 1997 Heretaunga Plains Groundwater Study describe the development of the plains over the past 250,000 years. That history gives the region its geological setting. It is not a measure of how old the water in a bottle is.

02 / THE AQUIFER

Artesian.
Pressure, explained.

Across the Heretaunga system, rainfall and river water help replenish underground gravels. The Ngaruroro River is a major source of recharge. Where less permeable layers confine the water, pressure builds.

In an artesian well, this pressure allows water to rise above the level at which it was reached. If the pressure is sufficient, water can flow at the surface without pumping. “Artesian” describes these groundwater conditions.

The regional aquifer is a shared, connected system. The map offers context for HBay’s home in Hawke’s Bay; it does not identify or certify an individual HBay well.

What do these numbers describe?

pH describes acidity or alkalinity. Total dissolved solids describes the combined concentration of dissolved substances. Calcium, magnesium and sodium are individual constituents within the water profile.
